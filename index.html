FULL DOCKET — EIGHT MORE REFERENCES

Practicing these tasks reliably improves the abilities they exercise — visual attention, processing speed, working-memory span, interference control. Whether those gains transfer broadly to everyday life is still debated. What is not debated: twenty minutes here exercises attention; twenty minutes of an infinite feed spends it.

  • Custom video-game training enhanced cognitive control in adults aged 60–85. Anguera et al. (2013), Nature 501, 97–101
  • Two months of platformer play induced measurable gray-matter growth in hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and cerebellum. Kühn et al. (2014), Molecular Psychiatry 19, 265–271
  • Strategy-game training attenuated cognitive decline measures in older adults. Basak et al. (2008), Psychology and Aging 23, 765–777
  • Executive functions — inhibition, working memory, cognitive flexibility — as a trainable triad. Diamond (2013), Annual Review of Psychology 64, 135–168
  • Working memory as temporary storage and manipulation — the frame Digit Span sits in. Baddeley (1992), Science 255, 556–559
  • Action-game players show faster processing speed without loss of accuracy. Dye, Green & Bavelier (2009), Current Directions 18, 321–326
  • Continuous performance / go–no-go as a standard probe of inhibitory control. Verbruggen & Logan (2008), Trends in Cognitive Sciences 12, 418–424
  • The caveat, on purpose: strong evidence for improvement on trained tasks; weak evidence for far transfer. This floor does not claim otherwise. Simons et al. (2016), Psychological Science in the Public Interest 17, 103–186
