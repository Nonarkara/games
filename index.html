📖 THE SCIENCE — WHAT TRAINING HERE CAN AND CANNOT DO

The games in this arcade are built on tasks with a real research record. The honest version of that record: practicing these tasks reliably improves the abilities they exercise — visual attention, processing speed, working-memory span, interference control. Whether those gains transfer broadly to everyday life is still debated, and you should read the skeptics too. What is not debated: twenty minutes here exercises your attention; twenty minutes of an infinite feed spends it.

  • Action video games measurably sharpen visual selective attention. Green & Bavelier (2003), Nature 423, 534–537
  • Dual n-back training improved working memory, with contested gains in fluid intelligence — the task behind our Dual N-Back. Jaeggi et al. (2008), PNAS 105, 6829–6833
  • Custom video-game training enhanced cognitive control in adults aged 60–85. Anguera et al. (2013), Nature 501, 97–101
  • Two months of platformer play induced measurable gray-matter growth in hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and cerebellum. Kühn et al. (2014), Molecular Psychiatry 19, 265–271
  • The interference task behind our Stroop game — a lab standard for attention and inhibition since 1935. Stroop (1935), J. Experimental Psychology 18, 643–662
  • Strategy-game training attenuated cognitive decline measures in older adults. Basak et al. (2008), Psychology and Aging 23, 765–777
  • Action-game players show faster processing speed without loss of accuracy. Dye, Green & Bavelier (2009), Current Directions in Psychological Science 18, 321–326
  • The caveat, on purpose: the major skeptical review found strong evidence for improvement on trained tasks and weak evidence for far transfer. This page does not claim otherwise. Simons et al. (2016), Psychological Science in the Public Interest 17, 103–186
